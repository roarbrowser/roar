Roar.

The open-source browser your agent can drive. Four quick steps, each one skippable; all of it is in Settings (⌘,) later.

Bring your stuff

Import bookmarks, pinned tabs and extensions from the browser you're leaving. From Arc, its spaces and folders come too.

Make Roar your default browser

Links you click in Mail, Slack or any other app open in Roar. macOS asks you to confirm. Until then, Roar reminds you at launch.

Connect your agent

Roar ships an MCP server, so Claude and other agents can open tabs, read pages, click and fill forms in the browser you're using. Connect adds Roar to that app's MCP config and keeps a .bak of the original.

Pick your sidebar modules

Each one sits as an icon at the bottom of the sidebar. Turn any of them on or off later in Settings.

Tips

⌘T opens one bar for addresses, searches, open tabs and pages from your history. Type > first to run a command such as “Copy page as Markdown”.

Share a page with your agent. Every live session in Agent sessions (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, Copilot CLI) has a Send tab button on its row and in its full view; in the command bar, type > and pick “Send tab to agent session…”. Roar saves the web page you're looking at as Markdown, with its title and address, and types a reference to that file into the session's prompt (@file) without sending it: you add your question and press Enter. To start fresh, press Chat in Agent sessions (no folder to pick; Roar keeps chats in its own folder), right-click a page and choose “Ask an Agent About This Page”, or leave “Include this tab” on when you press New or Chat: the page goes in as soon as the session starts. Pick which installed CLI they start beside it. Pin a session (or open its full view) to keep it in the sidebar above Tabs. Sessions in tmux or cmux take pages; very long pages are cut off, and ghost tabs are never shared. To ask a chat app instead, pick “Ask an LLM about page” in the command bar; Settings > General picks which apps it lists.

Spaces keep work and home apart, each with its own logins: switch with ⌃1–⌃9. ⌘\ puts two tabs side by side. Tabs you ignore drift to Archived; pinned ones stay.
