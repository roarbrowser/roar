Roar.

The open-source browser your agent can drive. Five quick steps, each one skippable; all of it is in Settings (⌘,) later.

Bring your stuff

Import bookmarks, pinned tabs and extensions from the browser you're leaving. From Arc, its spaces and folders come too.

Make Roar your default browser

Links you click in Mail, Slack or any other app open in Roar. macOS asks you to confirm. Until then, Roar reminds you at launch.

Connect your agent

Pick your AI and connect the tools you use, so they can see your pages and browse with Roar.

Connecting a coding agent like Claude Code also lists its sessions in the sidebar and tells you when one needs you or finishes. No agent tools here? Skip this step.

Talk instead of typing

Press ⌥⇧Space or the mic at the bottom of the sidebar, speak, then press it again. Your words go into the text field you're in: a web page, an AI chat or an agent's reply box.

Speech becomes text on this device, with NVIDIA's open Parakeet model; no audio leaves it. The model is about 470 MB and downloads once. Roar asks for the microphone the first time you dictate.

Pick your sidebar modules

Each one sits as an icon at the bottom of the sidebar. Turn any of them on or off later in Settings (the highlighted sliders). A ring on your space's dot means agents can use it; click the dot to turn that off.

Tips

⌘T is one bar for addresses, searches, open tabs and history. Start with > to run a command.

Share a page with your agent. The paperclip on an agent session sends it the page you're on, as Markdown. Code starts a coding agent session, with tabs attached if you like; Ask talks to a model directly.

Spaces keep work and home apart, each with its own logins (⌃1–⌃9). ⌘\ puts two tabs side by side. Idle tabs drift to Archived; pinned ones stay.
